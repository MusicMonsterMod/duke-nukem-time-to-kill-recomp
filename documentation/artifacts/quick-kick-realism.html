01 / What needs to feel right

The user's report

U: Downward Q looks as though the leg extends from Duke's shoulder. Jumping makes the torso visible and the kick appears to start above his belt. Wider FOV makes the mismatch harder to overlook.

The desired result

A readable hip-to-knee-to-boot chain when the body is visible. When looking forward or up, a convincing high kick may continue naturally out of frame. Entry, strike and recovery must flow through changes in pitch and jump phase.

Repositioning, re-angling and dynamic pose blending are candidates. No abrupt waist-angle switch or blanket body hiding is presumed necessary.

03 / How production work informs the investigation

Initial research seeds checked 28 October 2026. These support techniques and research questions, not a claim that every studio uses the same system. GDC abstracts have been read; full talks and frame-by-frame reference-game comparisons remain pending.

DICE / full-body first person

Mirror's Edge and Catalyst

DICE's production session abstract describes building a believable full-body experience and iterating animation prototypes. EA's Catalyst interview explains checking captured actions from the head-camera view so limbs and combat read clearly.

Application: judge the kick through Duke's actual camera across motion, not only by whether a mesh is anatomically placed in an external editor. These are first-person action references, not both conventional FPS examples.

Bungie / FPS animation

Destiny

The Art of First Person Animation for Destiny frames arms, weapon and camera motion around responsiveness and physicality within gameplay constraints.

Application: keep Q's response, attack timing and stable aiming while refining the visible action. The abstract does not disclose a hip solver or prove how Destiny renders its legs.

Epic / renderer architecture

Separate presentation and world representation

Epic's first-person rendering documentation describes custom first-person FOV and scale, a separate world representation for shadows/reflections, and per-vertex blending between world and first-person space, including feet connected to the ground.

Application: compare hybrid attachment with the current camera-relative kick. This is an architectural reference, not a drop-in feature for TTK or evidence about a named game's implementation.

Implemented technical example

Pitch-aware full-body posing

Lena Piquet's UE4 implementation uses additive poses driven by local pitch/yaw and discusses update ordering and animation/camera interaction.

Application: explore continuous pose blending and avoid one-frame disagreement. Its head-driven camera is not automatically appropriate for Duke's accepted stable eye. This is primary evidence for the author's implementation, not proprietary source evidence for the reference games it names.

06 / Solution decision

No-body default accepted. Further arm and kick polish remain queued.

Leading hypothesis: the display kick needs a coherent relationship to the visible hip, with pose adaptation when looking down or moving vertically. E1 tests whether fixed calibration is enough; E2-E4 test whether a continuous hybrid is justified. A mesh replacement is a fallback after demonstrating a limitation of the existing geometry.

The final decision must name the chosen transform/pose rule, rejected options and why, ownership/depth behavior, replay/reset requirements, measured cost, gameplay invariants and remaining visual risks. A screenshot that looks better at one pitch is insufficient.

07 / Acceptance and regression contract

  • Connected readable left-leg Q at default/60/80/90, down/level/up and continuous pitch sweeps. No shoulder origin, unexplained extra limb or abrupt pose switch.
  • Stand, walk/run, strafe, crouch, jump ascent/apex/descent/landing; chamber, strike, retract, repeated Q and interruption. Inspect Boot, pistol, Gatling and RPG; record actual equipment.
  • Sample 4:3, 16:9, 21:9 and 60/120 replay; inspect costume mappings and live costume coverage separately. Record unsupported or unvisited combinations.
  • Preserve exact reach, damage, hit-call arguments/count, attack timing and impact sounds. Preserve right-leg door 116 callbacks, accepted weapon framing and native gameplay matrices.
  • Preserve near-wall/floor occlusion, D14E1 corner guard, lighting/effects, reflection, third person and Vanilla. Check pause/load, view changes and kick cancellation for stale ownership.
  • Measure packet/allocation/replay behavior if prototypes change it. A requested frame-rate setting is not performance evidence.
